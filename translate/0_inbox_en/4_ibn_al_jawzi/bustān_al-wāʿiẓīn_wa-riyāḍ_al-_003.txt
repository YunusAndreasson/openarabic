It is narrated that Abu Sa'id al-Khudri, may God be pleased with him, said: I saw the accursed Iblis in a dream, hanging upside down. I was about to strike him with my staff, but he said to me, "O Abu Sa'id, do you not know that I fear neither the staff nor any weapon?" I said to him, "Then what do you fear, O accursed one?" He said, "I fear two things: the refuge-seeking of those who seek refuge in God, and the radiant light of the truly sincere."

It is also related that al-Junayd, may God have mercy on him, said: I saw Iblis in a dream, naked, toying with people. I said to him, "Have you no shame before these people?" The accursed one replied, "By God, do you call these people? If they were truly people, I would not toy with them the way children play with a ball." I said, "Then who are the real people?" He said, "Three men in the mosque of al-Shirazi — that is what the mosque was called. They have sickened my liver and wasted my body. Every time I approach them, they turn to God Most High, and I nearly burst into flames."

Al-Junayd said: I woke up while some of the night still remained, so I went out to the mosque the accursed one had mentioned and entered it. There I found three men sitting with their heads bowed into their patched cloaks. One of them said to me, "O Abu al-Qasim, must you believe everything you are told? Know, brother, that whoever seeks refuge in God from the accursed Satan has held firm to the straight path. For God, glorified be He, told us that the accursed Iblis declared, 'I will surely sit in wait for them on Your straight path.' God sent him as a highwayman blocking the road of faith, just as bandits block the worldly roads of Muslims. Iblis, may God curse him, is a highwayman of the hereafter, seeking to turn you away from truth and guidance. But when you seek refuge from him, he flees from you and cannot block your path of faith. God Most High has commanded His servants to say seventeen times in seventeen units of prayer — the number of obligatory cycles — 'Guide us to the straight path.' So how could the scheming of the accursed Satan ever harm you?"

"Know, brother, that the Frequented House was on earth until the time of Noah's flood, peace be upon him. It was preserved from drowning and saved from the deluge, then raised to the heavens. Yet the heart of the believer is greater than the Frequented House more than a million times over, and is therefore more deserving of God's protection — for the Frequented House is inhabited by the worship of angels, while the believer's heart is inhabited by the gaze of the Creator upon it. What an immense difference between the two!"

It is mentioned that Abu Sa'id said regarding God's words, "Over My servants you have no authority": it is as though He is saying, "Even if you have the power to cast them into disobedience to God, you have no power to prevent them from God's forgiveness." And another interpretation: if Satan has authority to plunge a servant into sin, then God's forgiveness has even greater authority to purify the servant from transgression. The power of Satan is never greater than the power of the Most Merciful's forgiveness in the hearts of the people of faith.
