Abd al-Muttalib had sought Amina's hand for his son Abdullah, and she was married to him. He stayed with her for a time, and something happened before she conceived the Messenger of God, peace be upon him.

Abu Fayyad al-Khath'ami reported: Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib passed by a woman of the Khath'am tribe named Fatima bint Murr. She was among the most beautiful, graceful, and chaste of people, and she had read the scriptures. The young men of Quraysh would seek her conversation. When she saw the light of prophethood on Abdullah's face, she said, "Young man, who are you?" He told her. She said, "Would you lie with me? I will give you a hundred camels." He looked at her and said, "As for what is forbidden, death is easier than that. And what is lawful, I cannot yet see clearly. So how could I do what you have in mind?" He then went to his wife Amina and was with her.

Afterward, he remembered the Khath'ami woman, her beauty, and what she had offered him, so he went to her. But he did not find in her the same eagerness she had shown before. He said, "Are you still offering what you said to me?" She replied, "That was a one-time thing — today, no." This became a well-known saying. She asked, "What did you do after you left me?" He said, "I was with my wife, Amina bint Wahb." She said, "By God, I have no desire for you out of lust. But I saw the light of prophethood on your face and wanted it to be through me. God, however, refused to place it anywhere but where He placed it."

When the young men of Quraysh heard what she had offered Abdullah and how he had refused her, they mentioned it to her, and she recited these verses: "I saw a sign appear in the sky, gleaming with the dark clouds of rain. Its light shone bright, illuminating all around it like the breaking of dawn. I saw it as an honor I might claim, but not every hand that strikes the flint produces fire. By God, no fair woman stripped you of what she stripped, nor does she even know it."

Jarir ibn Hazim reported from Abu Yazid al-Mada'ini that when Abdullah passed by the Khath'ami woman, she saw between his eyes a light shining up toward the sky. She said, "Would you be with me?" He said, "Yes — but let me first cast my pebbles at the pillar." So he went and cast his pebbles, then went to his wife Amina. Later he remembered the Khath'ami woman and went to her. She asked, "Were you with a woman after me?" He said, "Yes, Amina." She said, "Then I have no need of you. When you passed by, there was a light between your eyes reaching to the sky, but when you lay with her, it was gone." She then told him that Amina had conceived the best of the people of the earth.
