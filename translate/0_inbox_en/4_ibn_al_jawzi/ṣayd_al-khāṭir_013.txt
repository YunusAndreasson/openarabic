It may happen that a listener feels a surge of awakening upon hearing a sermon, only for hardness and heedlessness to return once the gathering ends. I reflected on the cause of this and came to understand it. Then I observed that people vary in this regard.

The general condition is that the heart cannot remain in the same state of alertness it had during the sermon, for two reasons. The first is that sermons are like lashes — they only sting at the moment of impact, not after. The second is that while listening to a sermon, a person is free of distractions, having set aside worldly concerns in both body and mind, listening with a present heart. But when they return to their preoccupations, those distractions pull them back with all their harms. So how could they possibly remain as they were?

This is a universal condition — yet those who are spiritually awake differ in how long the effect lasts. Among them are those who resolve without hesitation and press forward without looking back; if the pull of nature were to slow them down, they would cry out in protest, as Hanzalah said of himself: "Hanzalah has become a hypocrite!" Others are sometimes drawn by their nature toward heedlessness, and at other times called to action by the sermons they heard before — they are like a stalk of wheat swaying with the wind. And still others are only affected for as long as the sermon lasts, like water rolling off a smooth stone.

The forces pulling our nature toward this world are many, and they come from within, while the remembrance of the Hereafter is something external to our nature, arriving from outside. Some who lack understanding may think that the pull of the Hereafter is stronger, given the warnings they hear in the Quran. But it is not so, for our nature's inclination toward this world is like flowing water — it seeks to go downward; lifting it upward requires effort. This is why the allies of divine law responded: through enticement and warning, the forces of reason are strengthened. As for nature, its pulls are many, and the wonder is not that it overpowers — the wonder is that it is ever overpowered.

Whoever perceives with the eye of insight how things end while they are still beginning will reap the good and escape the harm. But whoever fails to see the consequences will be ruled by immediate sensation, and what he sought comfort in will bring him hardship, and what he hoped would give him rest will bring him exhaustion.

To clarify this regarding the future, consider the past: you have either disobeyed God in your life or obeyed Him. So where now is the pleasure of your disobedience? And where is the fatigue of your obedience? Both are long gone — each has departed with what it carried. If only sins, once they left, left us clean!

Let me make this even plainer: picture the moment of death, and consider the bitterness of regret over what was squandered. I will not even ask how it compares to the sweetness of fleeting pleasures — for that sweetness has already turned to bile, leaving only the bitterness of sorrow with nothing to counter it. Did you not know that everything is judged by its outcome? So keep your eye on the outcome and you will be safe, and do not follow the whims of your senses, or you will live to regret it.
