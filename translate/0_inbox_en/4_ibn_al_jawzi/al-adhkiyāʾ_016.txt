Among the stories passed down about Suwaybit ibn Sa'd ibn Harmala — who had fought at Badr — from Wahb ibn Abdullah ibn Zam'a, who said: Umm Salama told us: "Abu Bakr set out on a trading journey to Busra, a year before the death of the Messenger of God ﷺ, and with him were Nu'ayman and Suwaybit ibn Harmala, both of whom had fought at Badr. Nu'ayman was in charge of the provisions, and Suwaybit was a man fond of jokes. So he said to Nu'ayman, 'Feed me.' Nu'ayman replied, 'Not until Abu Bakr comes.' Suwaybit said, 'Then I will certainly get back at you.'

They passed by some people, and Suwaybit said to them, 'Will you buy a slave of mine?' They said, 'Yes.' He said, 'He is a slave who talks a lot, and he will tell you he is a free man. If, when he says that, you let him go, then don't ruin my slave for me.' They said, 'No — we will buy him from you.' So they bought him for ten young she-camels. Then they went to Nu'ayman and put a turban — or a rope — around his neck. Nu'ayman said, 'This man is mocking you! I am free, not a slave!' They said, 'He already told us you'd say that.' And they took him away.

Abu Bakr came and was told what happened. He went after the people, returned the camels to them, and took Nu'ayman back. When they later came before the Prophet ﷺ and told him the story, the Prophet ﷺ and his companions laughed about it for a whole year."

Among the stories passed down about Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan: Al-Mada'ini reported from Rabi'a ibn Najid, who said: Mu'awiya ibn Abi Sufyan was asked, "How far does your cunning reach?" He replied, "I have never fully trusted anyone."

Tha'lab said: On the day of Siffin, Mu'awiya glanced at one flank of his army and saw it wavering — he gave it a look, and it steadied. Then he glanced at the other flank and saw it wavering — he gave it a look, and it steadied. A man among his companions said to him, "Is this something you had been planning since the time of Uthman?" He said, "By God, this is something I had been planning since the time of Umar, may God be pleased with them all."

The author of the book said: We were told that a man came to Mu'awiya's doorkeeper and said, "Tell him that his brother — from the same father and mother — is at the door." Mu'awiya said, "I don't know this man." Then he said, "Let him in." When the man entered, Mu'awiya asked, "What kind of brother are you?" The man said, "A son of Adam and Eve." Mu'awiya said, "Boy, give him a dirham." The man protested, "You give your brother — from the same father and mother — just one dirham?" Mu'awiya replied, "If I gave every brother I have through Adam and Eve the same, the money would never have reached you."
