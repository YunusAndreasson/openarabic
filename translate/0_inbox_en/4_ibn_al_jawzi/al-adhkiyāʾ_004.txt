Wahb ibn Munabbih said: "I have found in what God revealed to His prophets that nothing is harder on Satan than a wise believer. He can wrestle with a hundred fools and drag them along until he rides their necks, leading them wherever he pleases. But when he wrestles with a wise believer, the man proves so difficult that Satan cannot get anything he wants from him."

Wahb also said: "Moving a mountain rock by rock and stone by stone is easier for Satan than contending with a wise believer. For when a man is a believer of sound mind and clear insight, he is heavier on Satan than mountains and harder than iron. Satan tries every trick on him, and when he cannot make him slip, he cries out: 'Woe to me! What do I want with this one? I am no match for him!' So he abandons him and turns to the fool, takes him captive, and seizes his reins until he delivers him to the disgraces that come swiftly in this world — flogging, stoning, head-shaving, the blackening of faces, amputation, and crucifixion. Two men may be equal in righteous deeds, yet the distance between them is like that between east and west — or greater — if one of them is wiser than the other."

Luqman said to his son: "My son, understand God well, for the most understanding of people toward God are the finest in deed. Satan flees from a wise person and cannot contend with him. My son, God has never been worshipped with anything better than the mind."

Luqman also said to his son: "My son, a person's mind is not complete until ten qualities are found in him: arrogance is not to be feared from him, and right guidance is hoped for in him; he takes from the world only his sustenance and gives away the rest of his wealth; humility is dearer to him than honor, and lowliness dearer to him than glory; he never tires of seeking knowledge his whole life, nor grows weary of people bringing their needs to him; he considers any small kindness from others to be much, and any great kindness from himself to be little. The tenth quality — the one that crowns his glory and elevates his name — is that he regards everyone in the world as better than himself and himself as the worst of them. If he sees someone better than him, he is glad and hopes to reach that person's level. If he sees someone worse, he says: 'Perhaps this one will be saved while I perish.' That is when the mind is made complete."

Luqman said: "The height of nobility and leadership is sound judgment. Whoever possesses sound judgment — it covers all his sins, mends all his faults, and earns the pleasure of his Lord."

Luqman said to his son: "My son, if you wish to befriend a man, anger him first. If he treats you fairly even in his anger, then take him as a friend. If not, beware of him."
