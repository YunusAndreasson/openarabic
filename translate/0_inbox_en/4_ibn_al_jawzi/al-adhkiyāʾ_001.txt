Al-Dahhak reported from Ibn Abbas regarding the verse "There was for Saba a sign in their dwelling place": He said their gardens never ceased producing for them, neither in winter nor in summer. But they were ungrateful for the blessings God had bestowed upon them, so He sent upon them the flood of the dam.

He unleashed upon the dam they had built — which held back their water supply — a rat with claws and fangs of iron. The first to learn of this was Abdullah ibn Amir al-Azdi. He went to the dam and saw the rat digging with iron claws and gnawing with iron fangs. He returned to his family, told his wife, and showed her what he had seen. Then he sent for his sons and said, "Do you see what we have seen?" They said, "Yes." He said, "This is a matter over which we have no power. All stratagems are useless against it, for the decree belongs to God, and He has ordained its destruction."

He brought a cat while the rat was digging, but the rat paid no attention to the cat. When the cat saw this, it turned and fled. Abdullah said to his sons, "Find a way to save yourselves." They said, "Father, how can we?" He said, "I will devise a plan for you."

He called his youngest son and said to him, "When I sit in the gathering today" — for people used to assemble around him and defer to his judgment — "once they have gathered, I will give your youngest brother an order, and he is to ignore it. When I rebuke him, he is to come at me and slap me. And none of you are to react against him. When the people in the gathering see that you did not react against your brother, none of them will dare to react either. Then I will swear an oath that cannot be expiated — that I will not remain among a people where the youngest of my sons rose up and slapped me and no one objected."

They said, "We will do it." When the people came to his gathering, he gave his son an order, and the boy ignored it. He ordered him again, and again the boy ignored it. He rebuked him, and the boy stood up and slapped him across the face. The people were astonished at the boy's audacity and lowered their heads, assuming the man's other sons would react. But when none of them did, the old man stood and swore that he would leave them and sell his estate, and that he would not remain among a people who did not stand up for him against his own son.

The people rose to apologize, saying, "We assumed your sons would react — that is what held us back." He said, "I have already sworn what you have heard, and there is no path now but departure." He put his properties up for sale — and people had always competed to acquire them — then he gathered his belongings and his family and left.

It was not long before the rat finished off the dam entirely and destroyed it. Then one night, after all eyes had closed in sleep, the people were suddenly struck by the flood. It swept away their livestock and their wealth and laid waste to their homes.
