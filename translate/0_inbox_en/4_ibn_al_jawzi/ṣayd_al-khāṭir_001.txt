His gatherings began to draw people in, and the residents of Baghdad crowded around him — elite and commoner alike — drinking deeply from his knowledge, his sermons, and his reminders. He became one of Baghdad's great luminaries, a source of pride that other cities envied. Visitors to Baghdad made a point of attending his gatherings, which had no equal anywhere in the Muslim world.

Here is the famous Andalusian traveler Muhammad ibn Ahmad ibn Jubayr, attending one of these gatherings and describing it beautifully in his celebrated travelogue. He writes:

"On the morning of the following Saturday, we witnessed the gathering of the Shaykh, the jurist, the singular Imam, Jamal al-Din Abu al-Fada'il ibn Ali al-Jawzi, held in front of his house on the riverbank on the eastern side — where he sits every Saturday. We witnessed the gathering of a man who was no ordinary person; as they say, 'within the fur lies all the game.' He was a wonder of his age, the delight of faith's eye, the chief of the Hanbalis, distinguished among scholars for holding the highest ranks — the imam of the congregation, the champion of his craft, universally acknowledged for his noble lead in eloquence and mastery, the one who held the reins of speech in both verse and prose, the diver who plunged into the sea of his own thought to retrieve precious pearls.

Among his most dazzling feats and greatest marvels was this: he would ascend the pulpit, and the reciters — numbering over twenty — would begin their recitation. Two or three of them would select a verse and chant it in sequence with melody and captivation. When they finished, another group of the same number would recite a second verse, and they would continue alternating verses from different chapters until they completed their recitation, having delivered verses so similar to one another that even the sharpest mind could scarcely count them or name them in order.

When they finished, this extraordinary imam would launch immediately into his sermon, pouring pearls of speech into the shells of listening ears. He wove the opening words of every verse the reciters had read into the body of his sermon, reproducing them in the exact order they had been recited — not one moved forward or back. He then concluded the sermon on the rhyme of the last verse recited. If the most brilliant person in that gathering had been asked to name the verses one by one in order, he would have failed — so how much more remarkable was it that this man strung them together extemporaneously, delivering a magnificent sermon with them on the spot?

Then, after finishing his sermon, he brought forth tender exhortations and luminous verses of remembrance that made hearts soar with longing and souls melt with burning desire — until the cries rose high, sobs echoed with gasping, and the repentant shouted aloud. They fell upon him the way moths fall upon a lamp, each one placing his forelock in the imam's hand to have it cut, while the imam passed his hand over their heads in prayer. Some fainted and had to be carried to him in people's arms.

We witnessed a scene that filled souls with repentance and remorse, and called to mind the terrors of the Day of Judgment. Had we crossed the surging sea and traversed the barren wilderness for no other purpose than to witness one of his gatherings, it would have been a profitable venture and a journey crowned with success."
