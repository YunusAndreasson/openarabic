Whoever reflects on the consequences of this world takes precaution, and whoever is certain of the length of the road prepares for the journey. How strange you are — you who believe in something and then forget it, who recognize the harm of a condition and yet walk into it, who fear people when God is more deserving of your fear! Your soul overpowers you in matters of doubt, yet you cannot overpower it in matters of certainty! The strangest of wonders: your delight in your own delusion, your heedlessness in your amusement of what has been stored up for you! You take your health for granted, forgetting how near illness is. You rejoice in your well-being, oblivious to the closeness of pain! The downfall of others has already shown you your own downfall, and the resting place of those who died before you has revealed where you too shall lie — yet the pursuit of your pleasures has distracted you from remembering your own ruin. It is as though you never heard the tales of those who came before, nor saw in the living what time does to all. If you do not know, then those are their homes — erased by the sweeping wind and the grave. How many a homeowner never left his house until he was lowered into his grave! How many a master of a palace saw it handed to his enemy once he was deposed! O you who drifts toward this end with every passing moment, yet acts like one who neither understands nor knows — how can the eye sleep contentedly when it does not know in which of the two abodes it will settle?

Whoever draws near to temptation, safety flees from him. Whoever claims patience is left to rely on himself alone. Many a glance was never returned. The things most deserving of restraint and control are the tongue and the eye. Beware — beware of trusting your resolve to abandon desire while still flirting with temptation, for desire is full of treachery! How many a brave warrior on the battlefield was assassinated — struck down by one he would have scorned to look at. Remember Hamza and Wahshi. *Look carefully, and do not chase every flash of lightning — for some lightning carries the thunderbolts of doom. Lower your gaze and find relief from a passion that clothes you in humiliation and disgrace. The trial of a man is indulging his soul, and desire begins with the wandering of the eye.*

The greatest punishment is when the one being punished does not even feel the punishment. Worse still is finding pleasure in what is itself a punishment — such as rejoicing in unlawful wealth, or being given free rein to sin. Whoever is in this state will never attain true obedience. I have studied the condition of most scholars and ascetics, and I have found them suffering punishments they do not perceive. Most of these stem from their pursuit of status. The scholar among them grows angry when his error is pointed out. The preacher is performing for his audience. The ascetic is a hypocrite or a show-off. The first of their punishments is that they turn away from the truth, preoccupied with people's approval. Among their subtler punishments is being stripped of the sweetness of intimate prayer and the joy of worship — all except believing men and believing women through whom God preserves the earth. Their inner lives are like their outward appearance, only more radiant. Their secret selves are like their public selves, only sweeter. Their aspirations reach the Pleiades, and higher still. If recognized, they conceal themselves. If shown a miracle, they deny it. People are caught up in their crops, while these souls are crossing their deserts. The plains of the earth love them, and the angels of heaven rejoice in them. We ask God for the grace to follow in their footsteps and to make us among their company.
