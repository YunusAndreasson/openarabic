Makhul related that Luqman the Wise was a black Nubian slave whom God, Most High, had granted wisdom. He belonged to a man of the Children of Israel who had purchased him for thirty and a half mithqals. Luqman worked for this man, and his master was fond of playing backgammon and gambling on it.

There was a flowing river by his master's door. One day, the master played a game on the condition that the loser would either drink all the water in the river or pay a ransom to the winner — and the winner could impose the same on the loser. Luqman's master lost.

The winner said to him, "Drink what is in the river, or else pay the ransom."

"Name your ransom," he replied.

"Your two eyes — I gouge them out — or everything you own."

"Give me the rest of today," the master pleaded.

"Granted," said the man.

That evening, the master sat grief-stricken and despondent. Then Luqman arrived carrying a bundle of firewood on his back. He greeted his master, set down his load, and came back to sit with him. Whenever the master saw Luqman, he would jest with him and listen to his words of wisdom, marveling at them. When Luqman sat down, he said to his master, "Why do I see you so troubled and sorrowful?"

The master turned away. Luqman asked a second time, and the master turned away again. He asked a third time, and the master turned away once more. Then Luqman said, "Tell me — perhaps I have a way out for you."

So the master told him the whole story. Luqman said, "Do not despair, for I do have a way out for you."

"What is it?" he asked.

Luqman said, "When the man comes and tells you to drink what is in the river, say to him: 'Shall I drink the water between the two banks, or the floodwater as well?' He will say, 'Drink what is between the two banks.' Then say to him, 'Hold back the floodwater from me so I can drink what is between the banks.' He will not be able to hold back the floodwater, and you will have freed yourself from what you owed him."

The master recognized the truth of this and his spirits lifted. The next morning the man came and said, "Fulfill my condition."

"Very well," said the master. "Shall I drink the water between the two banks, or the floodwater too?"

"Only what is between the two banks," the man said.

"Then hold back the floodwater from me."

"How could I possibly do that?" the man replied.

And so the master won the argument. After that, he set Luqman free.
