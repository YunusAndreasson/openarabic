Among what is reported from Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, may God be pleased with him: Thabit narrated from Anas, who said: When the Messenger of God ﷺ made the Hijra, he would ride while Abu Bakr sat behind him. Abu Bakr knew the road well from his frequent travels to Syria. They would pass by people who would ask, "Who is this man in front of you, Abu Bakr?" He would reply, "A guide who guides me."

Al-Hasan narrated: When the Messenger of God ﷺ and Abu Bakr left the cave, everyone who recognized Abu Bakr and met them would ask, "Who is this with you, Abu Bakr?" He would say, "A guide showing me the way." And by God, Abu Bakr spoke the truth.

Al-Hasan narrated: When the Messenger of God ﷺ and Abu Bakr came out to the people, he said, "God has given a servant the choice between this world and what is with Him, and that servant chose what is with God." Abu Bakr wept, and we were puzzled by his weeping — that the Messenger of God had merely spoken of a servant who was given a choice. But the Messenger of God ﷺ was himself that servant, and Abu Bakr was the most knowledgeable of us about him.

Among what is reported from Umar ibn al-Khattab, may God be pleased with him: Aslam narrated from his father, who said: Some garments from Yemen were brought to Umar ibn al-Khattab, and he began distributing them among the people. He noticed one garment of poor quality and said, "What do I do with this? If I give it to someone, they will refuse it once they see the defect." So he took it, folded it, and placed it under where he sat, with just the edge showing, then set out the other garments before him and continued distributing them. Al-Zubayr ibn al-Awwam walked in while he was doing this and began eyeing that garment. He asked, "What is this garment?" Umar said, "Leave it alone." Al-Zubayr said, "What is it? What is it? What's the matter with it?" Umar said, "Leave it." Al-Zubayr said, "Then give it to me." Umar said, "You won't be satisfied with it." Al-Zubayr said, "I am satisfied with it." So once Umar had secured his word and made him agree to accept it without returning it, he tossed it to him. When al-Zubayr took it and examined it, he found it was of poor quality and said, "I don't want it." Umar said, "Too late — it's done." He made him keep it and refused to take it back.

Nafi' reported from Ibn Umar, who said: While Umar, may God be pleased with him, was sitting, he noticed a man and said, "I once had sharp intuition, and I'd have no judgment left if this man hasn't practiced some form of divination." He said, "Bring him to me." They brought the man, and Umar asked, "Did you used to practice divination?" The man said, "Yes."

It is also reported that Umar, may God be pleased with him, went out one night patrolling Medina and saw a fire burning in a tent. He stopped and called out, "O people of the light!" — disliking to say "O people of the fire." This was a mark of extraordinary wit.

It is also reported that he said to a man who had just married, "Has anything happened yet?" The man replied, "No — may God not prolong your life." Umar said, "You have learned, yet you haven't truly learned! Why didn't you say, 'No — and may God prolong your life'?"
