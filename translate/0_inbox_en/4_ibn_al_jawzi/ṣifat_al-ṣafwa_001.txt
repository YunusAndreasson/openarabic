Halimah bint al-Harith, the wet-nurse of the Messenger of God ﷺ, of the Sa'diyyah clan, said: I set out with a group of women from Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr ibn Hawazin, seeking nurslings in Mecca. I rode a pale she-donkey of mine that had slowed the whole caravan. We had set out in a year of drought that had left us with nothing — my husband al-Harith ibn 'Abd al-'Uzza and I. We had an old she-camel with us, but by God, she would not yield us a single drop of milk. I also had an infant of ours, and by God, we could not sleep at night because of his crying — there was no milk in my breast to satisfy him, and no milk from our she-camel to nourish him. Still, we held on to the hope of rain and relief.

When we arrived in Mecca, there was not a woman among us who was not offered the Messenger of God ﷺ, but each one refused him. We had been hoping for generosity from the father of whatever child we nursed, and he was an orphan ﷺ, so we said, "What can his mother possibly do for us?" We kept refusing, until every one of my companions had taken a nursling except me.

I hated the thought of returning empty-handed while all my companions had taken infants. So I said to my husband al-Harith, "By God, I will go back and take that orphan." I went to him, took him, and brought him back to our camp. My husband said, "You took him?" I said, "Yes — I could find no one else." He said, "You have done well. Perhaps God will place blessing in him."

By God, no sooner had I placed him in my lap than my breasts swelled with all the milk he needed. He drank until he was full, and his foster-brother drank until he was full. Then my husband al-Harith went to our old she-camel in the night, and there she was, her udder brimming — we milked as much as we wanted. He drank until he was satisfied, and I drank until I was satisfied. We spent the most wonderful night — full and content.

My husband said, "By God, Halimah, I can only think you have taken a blessed soul. Our children have slept, and we have all had our fill."

Then we set out. By God, my she-donkey raced ahead of the entire caravan, leaving them all behind until not one of them could keep up with her. They called out, "Slow down for us, daughter of al-Harith! Is this not the same donkey you came on?" I would say, "Yes, by God, it is." And they would say, "Something extraordinary is happening."

We arrived at our homes in the territory of Banu Sa'd ibn Bakr — the most barren land of God's earth. Yet, by Him in whose hand is Halimah's soul, they would send out their flocks in the morning and I would send out my flock, and my sheep would return with udders full and bellies round, while theirs would come back starving and dry, without a drop of milk. We drank as much milk as we pleased, while not another soul in the settlement could milk a single drop or find any. They would say to their shepherds, "Curse you — why don't you graze where Halimah's shepherd grazes?" So they would take their flocks to the very same valley where mine grazed, yet their sheep would return hungry and dry, while mine came back bursting with milk.
