He remained in that state from the year ninety to the year five hundred and ninety-five, when he was released and returned to Baghdad. A great multitude came out on the day of his arrival to welcome him, and the people of Baghdad rejoiced immensely. It was announced that he would hold his session on Saturday. The people prayed the Friday prayer and then crossed over to secure their places at the gathering site near the tomb of the Caliph's mother. That night, heavy rain fell and flooded the streets. Attendants and workers were brought in during the night to clean the seating area and spread fine gravel and mats over it. During the rain, people took shelter at the tomb of Ma'ruf al-Karkhi beneath the covered passageway until the rain subsided. Then, on Saturday morning, the Sheikh took his seat. Crowds poured in — scholars from the madrasas, Sufis, and elders of the lodges all attended. The open grounds were so packed that the Sheikh's voice could not reach those at the back.

The Caliph restored the Sheikh to Baghdad, bestowed robes of honor upon him, and he sat by the tomb of the Caliph's mother to preach. He recited:

> We suffered long from separation, yet when we reunited, it was as though we never suffered at all.
> We raged at what the nights had wrought, but they kept at us until we were content.
> We found joy in reunion — how long we had suffered from the cups of rejection, how long we had wasted away.
> Whoever has not lived again after death — know that we, after we had died, came back to life.

The Sheikh continued in his former habit of preaching, spreading knowledge, and writing until his death.

Ibn al-Jawzi passed away after an illness lasting five days, on the eve of Friday between the Maghrib and Isha prayers, on the thirteenth of the blessed month of Ramadan in the year five hundred and ninety-seven, in a house of his near the tomb of Ma'ruf al-Karkhi on the western side of the City of Peace, Baghdad.

All sources agree that the day of his death was a momentous one in Baghdad. Hearts trembled at the news, markets were shut, and the call went out for funeral prayers on both sides of the city. His bier was carried aloft on the heads of the people and taken to the Mosque of al-Mansur for the prayer. His son Abu al-Qasim Ali led the prayer, and the mosque, vast as it was, could not contain the crowd, so the funeral prayer was performed twice. He was then carried to the cemetery of Bab Harb and buried there, near Imam Ahmad, may God have mercy on him.

His grandson Abu al-Muzaffar said: "My grandfather instructed that the following be inscribed on his grave:

> O You who pardons abundantly the one whose sins are many before Him — the sinner has come to You, hoping for forgiveness for what his hands have done. I am a guest, and a guest deserves to be treated with kindness."
