Abu Ishaq said: If you hear that a rich man has become poor, believe it. If you hear that a poor man has become rich, believe it. If you hear that a living man has died, believe it. But if you hear that a fool has gained wisdom, do not believe it.

Abu Yusuf the Judge said: There are three things — believe two of them, but do not believe the third. If you are told that a man who was with you stepped behind a wall and died, believe it. If you are told that a poor man traveled to another land and acquired wealth, believe it. But if you are told that a fool traveled to another land and acquired wisdom, do not believe it.

Al-Awza'i reported that it was said to Jesus son of Mary, peace be upon him: "O Spirit of God, can you raise the dead?" He said: "Yes, by God's permission." They said: "And can you heal the blind?" He said: "Yes, by God's permission." They said: "Then what is the cure for foolishness?" He said: "That is the one thing that has defeated me."

Ja'far ibn Muhammad said: Educating a fool is like pouring water on the roots of a bitter gourd — the more you water it, the more bitter it grows.

Al-Ma'mun said: Do you know what happened between me and the Commander of the Faithful, Harun al-Rashid? I had committed some offense against him, so I went in to greet him. He said: "Get out of my sight, you fool!" I left in anger and did not visit him for days. Then he sent me a note that read: "I wonder, now that this estrangement has gone on so long — was the fault yours or was it mine? If you wronged us, may God forgive you; and if I wronged you, then forgive me." So I went to him, and he said: "If the fault was ours, we have asked your forgiveness; and if it was yours, we have forgiven it." I said to him: "You called me a fool. Had you called me reckless, it would have been easier for me." He asked: "What is the difference between the two?" I said: "Recklessness is something picked up from women — it clings to a man from keeping their company too long, and once he parts from them and keeps the company of real men, it leaves him. But foolishness is inborn."

One of the sages recited: "Treating the ailments of the body is a lighter matter, when it falls ill, than treating the ailments of the mind."

We have already noted that foolishness is a corruption of the intellect or the mind, and whatever is rooted in the very essence of a person's nature is inborn — no amount of discipline can remedy it. Discipline and training only benefit those whose essential nature is sound, by warding off corrupting influences. Moreover, people differ in intellect, in its substance, and in the measure they have been given of it, and so foolishness, too, varies in degree.

Ibrahim al-Nazzam was asked: "What is the definition of foolishness?" He replied: "You have asked me about something that has no limit."

Ali said: "There is no one who does not have a touch of foolishness, and it is by that touch that he is able to go on living."

Wahb ibn Munabbih said: "God created Adam with a measure of foolishness, for without it, life would have given him no pleasure."

Mutarrif said: "If I swore an oath, I would hope to be truthful in saying that there is no one among people who is not foolish in what lies between him and God Almighty." He also used to say: "There is no one who is not foolish in what lies between him and his Lord, except that some foolishness is lighter than other foolishness."

Sufyan al-Thawri said: "Man was created foolish so that he might enjoy living."

And one of them recited: "By your life, nothing you fail to attain is truly a loss — the real disparity lies in people's minds."
