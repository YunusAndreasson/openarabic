The Almighty, blessed and exalted, says: "Who remains, O Angel of Death?" — though He, exalted be He, knows best. The Angel replies: "My Lord and Master, You know best who remains. Only Your weak servant, the Angel of Death, remains." The Almighty says: "By My glory and majesty, I shall make you taste what I made My servants taste. Go forth between Paradise and Hellfire, and die." So he goes forth between Paradise and Hellfire and lets out a cry so terrible that, had God not already taken the lives of all creation, every last one of them would have perished from its force. And so he dies.

The heavens are left empty of their angels, their celestial spheres stilled. The earth is left desolate — devoid of its humans and jinn, its birds and crawling creatures, its beasts and livestock. Sovereignty belongs to God alone, the One, the Overpowering, who created the night and the day. Not a companion is seen, not a whisper is heard. All movement has ceased, all voices have fallen silent, and the earth and heavens have been emptied of their inhabitants.

Then God, blessed and exalted, turns His attention to the world and says: "O world, where are your rivers? Where are your trees? Where are your inhabitants? Where are your thriving cities? Where are the kings and the sons of kings? Where are the tyrants and the sons of tyrants? Where are those who consumed My provision, reveled in My blessings, yet worshipped other than Me? To whom does sovereignty belong today?" No one answers. So He, exalted, declares: "Sovereignty belongs to God, the One, the Overpowering."

The Almighty, majestic is His glory, looks upon His servants — all dead, some lying fallen upon their cheeks, others decaying in their graves. Then He says again: "O world, where are your rivers? Where are your trees? Where are your inhabitants? Where are your thriving cities? Where are the kings? Where are the tyrants? To whom does sovereignty belong today?" No one answers. So God, exalted, declares: "To God, the One, the Overpowering."

The earth and heavens remain with no one in them who speaks and no one who breathes, for as long as God wills. It is said this lasts forty days — the span between the two blasts of the trumpet. Then, after that, God, blessed and exalted, sends down from the seventh heaven, from a sea called the Sea of Life, a water that our Lord rains down for forty years. That water splits the earth open and seeps beneath it, reaching the decayed bones, which sprout from that water just as crops sprout from rain. Just as He brings forth vegetation through rain, so too He brings forth the dead through the water of life. The bones, veins, flesh, and hair reassemble, each limb returning to its rightful place as it was in the life of this world. The bodies are made whole by the power of the Almighty, majestic is His glory — yet they remain without souls.
