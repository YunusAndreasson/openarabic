Zayd ibn Aslam narrated that a man said to Hudhayfah, "O Hudhayfah, we complain to God about your advantage over us — you were companions of the Messenger of God. You reached him and we did not; you saw him and we never did." Hudhayfah replied, "And we complain to God about your faith in him without ever having seen him. By God, you have no idea, nephew, how you would have been had you lived in his time.

I remember being with the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, on the night of the Trench — a cold, dark, rainy night. Abu Sufyan and his forces had encamped on the open ground. The Messenger of God said, 'Is there a man who will go and bring us news of the enemy? God will admit him to Paradise.' Not one of us stood. Then he said, 'Is there a man who will go and bring us news of the enemy? God will make him a companion of Abraham on the Day of Resurrection.' By God, not one of us stood. Then he said, 'Is there a man who will go and bring us news of the enemy? God will make him my companion on the Day of Resurrection.' By God, still no one stood.

Then Abu Bakr said, 'O Messenger of God, send Hudhayfah.' So the Messenger of God called out, 'O Hudhayfah!' I answered, 'Here I am, O Messenger of God — may my father and mother be your ransom.' He asked, 'Will you go?' I said, 'By God, it is not death I fear, but I am afraid of being captured.' He said, 'You will not be captured.' I said, 'Command me as you wish, O Messenger of God.' He said, 'Go and slip in among the enemy. Go to the Quraysh and say: O people of Quraysh, when morning comes, the others will say, "Where are the Quraysh? Where are the leaders? Where are the chiefs?" — and they will push you to the front so you bear the brunt of the fighting and the killing falls on you. Then go to Qays and say: O people of Qays, when morning comes, they will say, "Where are the seasoned horsemen? Where are the cavalry?" — and they will push you forward so you take the first blows and the killing falls on you.'

So I set off and slipped in among the enemy. I warmed myself at their fires alongside them and began spreading the words the Prophet had instructed me to say. Then, in the bitter cold before dawn, Abu Sufyan stood up, swore by al-Lat and al-Uzza and his idols, and said, 'Let every man check who is sitting beside him.' There was a man next to me warming himself at the fire, so I seized his hand before he could seize mine and said, 'Who are you?' He answered, 'I am so-and-so, son of so-and-so.' I said, 'Very well.'

When dawn drew near, they called out, 'Where are the Quraysh? Where are the chiefs?' and the Quraysh said, 'Give us that talk you brought us last night.' Then they called, 'Where are the Banu Kinanah? Where are the archers?' and they too said, 'Give us that talk you brought us last night.' So suspicion and discord overtook them. That night God sent a wind against them that left no tent standing and no vessel upright. I saw Abu Sufyan himself leap onto one of his hobbled camels and try to drag it along, unable to get it to its feet.

I came back to the Messenger of God and began telling him about Abu Sufyan, and he laughed until his back teeth showed — and I kept gazing at those teeth of his as he laughed."
