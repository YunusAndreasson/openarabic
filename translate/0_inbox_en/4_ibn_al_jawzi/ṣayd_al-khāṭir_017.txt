A sensible person must prepare for their departure, for they never know when their Lord's decree will come upon them suddenly, nor when they will be summoned. I have seen so many people deluded by their youth, forgetting how many of their peers have already perished. Long hopes distracted them. Sometimes even the pure scholar tells himself: "I will busy myself with knowledge today and act on it tomorrow!" So he grows lenient with his mistakes under the pretext of rest, delays his readiness on the promise of future repentance, and does not shy away from backbiting or listening to it. He earns doubtful gains, hoping to erase them later through piety, forgetting that death may strike without warning. The truly sensible person gives every moment its due of what is required of him — if death takes him by surprise, he is found ready; and if he is granted more time, he only grows in goodness.

A thought occurred to me about the severe misfortunes and great tribulations that befall so many people, reaching the utmost extremity of hardship. I said to myself: "Glory be to God! God is the most generous of the generous, and generosity calls for leniency — so what explains these punishments?" I reflected and saw that many people exist as though they do not exist at all. They never examine the proofs of God's oneness, nor consider His commands and prohibitions. Rather, they follow their habits like cattle — if religious law happens to align with their desires, fine; if not, they follow their own whims. Once they get their money, they do not care whether it came from lawful or unlawful means. If prayer is easy for them, they perform it; if not, they abandon it. Among them are those who commit grave sins openly, despite having some awareness of what is forbidden. Sometimes a scholar among them has deep knowledge, yet his sins only grow worse. I realized then that the punishments, however great, still fall short of their crimes. Yet when a punishment strikes to purify a sin, their crier shouts: "What sin could this possibly be for?!" — forgetting what he has done, things that would make the earth tremble. An old man may be humiliated in his later years until hearts pity him, not realizing that it is because he neglected God's rights in his youth. So whenever you see someone being punished, know that it is for sins.

Whoever wishes to purify their circumstances must strive to purify their deeds. God Almighty said: *"Had they stayed true to the path, We would have given them water in abundance."* Abu Sulayman al-Darani said: "Whoever purifies, things are purified for him; whoever muddies, things are muddied upon him. Whoever does well in his night is rewarded in his day, and whoever does well in his day is rewarded in his night." There was a sheikh who would go around the gatherings saying: "Whoever would love for well-being to last, let him be mindful of God." Al-Fudayl ibn Iyad used to say: "I disobey God, and I see the effect of it in the temperament of my riding animal and my servant."

Know that one who is numbed feels no blow — only the person who holds himself to account recognizes what has increased and what has diminished. Whenever you see turbulence in your condition, recall a blessing you never gave thanks for, or a slip you committed. Beware of blessings taking flight and calamities arriving without warning. Do not be deceived by how wide the carpet of God's forbearance is spread — for it may be rolled up in an instant.
