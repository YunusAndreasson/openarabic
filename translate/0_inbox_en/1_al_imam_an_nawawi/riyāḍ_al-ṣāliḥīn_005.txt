On the authority of Abu Ishaq Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas Malik ibn Uhayb ibn Abd Manaf ibn Zuhrah ibn Kilab ibn Murrah ibn Ka'b ibn Lu'ayy al-Qurashi al-Zuhri — may God be pleased with him — one of the ten who were promised Paradise, he said: The Messenger of God, peace be upon him, came to visit me during the year of the Farewell Pilgrimage on account of a severe illness that had afflicted me. I said, "O Messenger of God, my illness has reached the state you see, and I am a man of wealth with no heir except a single daughter. Shall I give two-thirds of my wealth in charity?" He said, "No." I said, "Then half, O Messenger of God?" He said, "No." I said, "Then a third, O Messenger of God?" He said, "A third — and even a third is a lot. It is better for you to leave your heirs wealthy than to leave them destitute, begging from people. And you will never spend anything seeking the pleasure of God without being rewarded for it — even the morsel you place in your wife's mouth." I said, "O Messenger of God, will I be left behind after my companions?" He said, "You will not be left behind and do any deed seeking the pleasure of God without it raising you in rank and station. And perhaps you will live on so that some people will benefit from you and others will be harmed by you. O God, let my companions' migration stand and do not turn them back on their heels — but poor Sa'd ibn Khawlah." The Messenger of God, peace be upon him, felt sorry for him because he had died in Mecca.

On the authority of Abu Yazid Ma'n ibn Yazid ibn al-Akhnas — may God be pleased with them all, for he, his father, and his grandfather were all Companions — he said: My father Yazid had set aside some gold coins to give in charity, and he left them with a man in the mosque. I came and took them, then brought them to him. He said, "By God, it was not you I intended them for!" So we took our dispute to the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, and he said, "You have the reward of what you intended, O Yazid, and you may keep what you took, O Ma'n."

On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may God be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of God, peace be upon him, said, "God does not look at your bodies or your appearances, but He looks at your hearts and your deeds."

On the authority of Abu Musa Abdullah ibn Qays al-Ash'ari, may God be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of God, peace be upon him, was asked about a man who fights out of bravery, one who fights out of tribal pride, and one who fights to show off — which of these is in the cause of God? The Messenger of God, peace be upon him, said, "Whoever fights so that the word of God is supreme — he is the one fighting in the cause of God."
