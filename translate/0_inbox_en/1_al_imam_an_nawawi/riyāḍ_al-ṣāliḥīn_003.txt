Abu Sa'id Sa'd ibn Malik ibn Sinan al-Khudri reported that the Prophet of God, peace be upon him, said: "Among those who came before you, there was a man who had killed ninety-nine people. He asked about the most knowledgeable person on earth and was directed to a monk. He went to him and said that he had killed ninety-nine people — was there any chance of repentance for him? The monk said no, so the man killed him too, completing a hundred.

Then he asked again about the most knowledgeable person on earth and was directed to a scholar. He told him that he had killed a hundred people — was there any chance of repentance for him? The scholar said: 'Yes, and who could stand between you and repentance? Go to such-and-such land, for there you will find people who worship God, the Exalted. Worship God with them, and do not return to your own land, for it is a land of evil.'

So the man set out, but when he had reached the midpoint of the journey, death came to him. The angels of mercy and the angels of punishment began to dispute over him. The angels of mercy said: 'He came as a penitent, turning his heart sincerely toward God.' The angels of punishment said: 'He never did a single good deed.' Then an angel came to them in human form, and they made him their arbiter. He said: 'Measure the distance between the two lands — whichever one he is closer to, that is where he belongs.' They measured, and found him closer to the land he had been heading toward, so the angels of mercy took his soul."

In another authentic narration: "He was closer to the righteous village by a handspan, so he was counted among its people." And in yet another narration: "God, the Exalted, commanded the land he was leaving to stretch farther away and the land he was heading toward to draw nearer. Then He said: 'Measure the distance between them.' They found him closer to the righteous land by a handspan, and he was forgiven." In another narration: "He had leaned his chest forward toward it."

Abu Hamza Anas ibn Malik al-Ansari, the servant of the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, reported that the Messenger of God said: "God is more joyful over the repentance of His servant than one of you would be upon finding his camel after losing it in a barren desert."

In a narration by Muslim: "God is more intensely joyful over the repentance of His servant when he turns to Him than one of you who was riding his camel in a barren desert when it escaped from him, carrying all his food and drink. He gave up hope, went to a tree, and lay down in its shade, having lost all hope of finding his camel. Then, while he was in that state, there it was — standing right beside him! He grabbed its reins and then cried out in overwhelming joy: 'O God, You are my servant and I am Your lord!' — blundering in his words out of sheer joy."
