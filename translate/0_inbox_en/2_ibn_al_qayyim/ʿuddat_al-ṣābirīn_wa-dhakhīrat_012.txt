The third case is that the battle goes back and forth between the two armies — sometimes in his favor, sometimes against him — with victories coming often at times and rarely at others. This is the condition of most believers who mix good deeds with bad ones. On the Day of Judgment, the outcome will correspond exactly to these three conditions: some people will enter Paradise and never enter the Fire; some will enter the Fire and never enter Paradise; and some will enter the Fire and then be admitted to Paradise. These three states also mirror people's conditions regarding health and illness: some have a constitution strong enough to overpower their disease, and strength prevails; others are overpowered by their disease, and illness prevails; and others go back and forth between their illness and their strength, alternating between health and sickness.

**Chapter:** Among people, some endure with great effort and hardship, while others endure with little strain. The first is like a man who wrestles a powerful opponent and cannot defeat him without exhausting struggle. The second is like a man who wrestles a weak opponent and throws him down with ease. This is how the struggle between the soldiers of the Most Merciful and the soldiers of Satan plays out — and whoever defeats Satan's soldiers has defeated Satan himself.

Abdullah ibn Mas'ud, may God be pleased with him, said: "A man among the humans met a man among the jinn and wrestled him, and the human threw him down. He said, 'Why do I see you so frail?' The jinn replied, 'Among my kind, I am actually quite strong.' They asked, 'Was it Umar ibn al-Khattab?' He replied, 'Who else could it be but Umar?'"

One of the Companions said: "A believer wears out his devil the way one of you wears out his camel on a journey." Ibn Abi al-Dunya reported from some of the early Muslims that one devil met another and asked, "Why do I see you so haggard?" He replied, "I am with a man who mentions God's name when he eats, so I cannot eat with him; who mentions God's name when he drinks, so I cannot drink with him; and who mentions God's name when he enters his house, so I spend the night outside." The other said, "But I am with a man who does not mention God's name when he eats, so I eat right alongside him; who does not mention God's name when he drinks, so I drink with him; who does not mention God's name when he enters his house, so I enter with him; and who does not mention God's name when he lies with his wife, so I lie with her too."

Whoever makes patience a habit, his enemy will fear him. And whoever finds patience difficult, his enemy will set his sights on him and will soon get what he wants from him.
