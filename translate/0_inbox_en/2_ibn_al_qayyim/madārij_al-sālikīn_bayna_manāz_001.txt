The landmarks of the Quran have faded in their hearts, so they no longer recognize them. Its sacred sites have been buried in their midst, so they no longer tend to them. Its banners and standards have fallen from their hands, so they no longer raise them. Its luminous stars have set below the horizons of their souls, and so they no longer love them. Its sun has been eclipsed by the gathering darkness of their opinions and doctrines, so they no longer see it. They stripped the texts of revelation of the authority of truth and removed them from the governance of certainty. They launched against them the raids of false interpretations, and ambush after ambush continues to emerge from their armies against them. The texts descended upon them as a guest descends upon base and ignoble hosts, and they treated them with none of the reverence and honor they deserve. They received them from afar, but only to push them back by the chest, saying: "You have no passage with us, and if you must, then only in passing." They reduced the texts to the status of a caliph in these times — his name appears on the coin and is mentioned in the sermon, yet he holds no real authority or power. In their eyes, the one who holds fast to the Book and the Sunnah is a mere literalist, shortchanged of reason, while the one who blindly follows contradictory opinions and crumbling ideas is, to them, the virtuous and accepted one. The people of the Book and the Sunnah, who place its texts above all else, are in their view ignorant and deficient.

By God, they have been denied arrival at the truth because they turned away from the path of revelation and squandered the foundations. They clung to tails that had no heads, and these failed them at the very moment they needed them most. Their means were severed from them precisely when they were most in need. Until, when the graves are overturned and what lies within the hearts is laid bare, and every people's harvest — what they truly gathered — becomes distinct, and the reality of what they believed is exposed to them, and they arrive before what they sent ahead, and despair seizes them at the reaping when they behold the yield of what they sowed.

How intense the anguish when the one upon falsehood sees his toil and labor turned to scattered dust! How immense the calamity when he discovers that the flashes of his hopes were mere mirages, and his aspirations nothing but lying delusions! What does one whose inner self is wrapped in innovation, caprice, and blind partisanship to opinions expect from his Lord on the Day when secrets are laid bare? And what excuse has the one who cast the two revelations behind his back on a Day when no excuses will avail the wrongdoers? Does the one who turns away from the Book of his Lord and the Sunnah of His Messenger truly think he will be saved from his Lord by the opinions of men? Or that he will escape the might of God through an abundance of debates and arguments, varieties of analogies and forms of reasoning? Or through mystical allusions, ecstatic utterances, and flights of imagination? Far from it, by God — he has entertained the most false of assumptions, and his soul has promised him the most patent of impossibilities.
