The drive of religion, in relation to the drive of desire, manifests in three states.

The first is when the force of religion prevails and dominates, driving back the army of desire in chains. This is only achieved through unwavering patience. Those who reach this rank are the victorious in this world and the next. They are the ones who said, "Our Lord is God," and then stood firm. They are the ones to whom the angels say at the moment of death, "Do not fear, do not grieve, and rejoice in the Paradise you were promised. We are your allies in this life and in the Hereafter." They are the ones who earned God's companionship alongside the patient, and they are the ones who strove in God's cause as it truly deserves, and He singled them out with His guidance above all others.

The second state is when the force of desire overpowers and overwhelms, and the drive of religion collapses entirely. The wretched soul surrenders to Satan and his soldiers, who lead it wherever they please. In this state, a person falls into one of two conditions. The first is that he becomes one of Satan's foot soldiers and followers — this is the condition of the weak and helpless. The second is that Satan actually becomes one of his soldiers — this is the condition of the powerful, tyrannical sinner, or the charismatic innovator with a following. As the poet said: "I was a soldier in the army of Iblis, but my station rose until Iblis became a soldier in mine." In this case, Iblis and his forces become the person's helpers and followers. These are the people whom wretchedness has overtaken, who have traded the Hereafter for the life of this world. They reached this state only because they went bankrupt of patience. This is the state of utter affliction, the depths of misery, the worst of fates, and the gloating of enemies. The army of such people consists of deception, trickery, false hopes, delusion, endlessly postponing good deeds, clinging to long-term worldly ambitions, and preferring the immediate over the everlasting. It is about such a person that the Prophet said: "The truly incapable one is he who lets his soul follow its whims and then places empty wishes upon God."

The people of this condition are of many kinds. Among them is the one who wages war against God and His Messenger, striving to undo what the Messenger brought, blocking God's path and doing his utmost to twist and distort it so as to turn people away from it. Among them is the one who simply turns away from what the Messenger brought, consumed entirely by his worldly life and its pleasures. Among them is the hypocrite with two faces, who profits from both disbelief and faith. And among them is the shameless jester who has squandered his every breath in frivolity, amusement, and play.
