This is what Sheikh Abdul Qadir — may God have mercy on him — alluded to, as reported from him. He explained that many great men, when they reach the matter of divine decree and predestination, hold back and fall silent. "But as for me," he said, "a window was opened for me into it, and I contended against the decrees of God with God's truth and for God's sake. The true man is one who contends against destiny, not one who simply submits to it."

What the Sheikh — may God have mercy on him — described is indeed what God and His Messenger commanded. However, many people have misunderstood this point. They may witness the sins and transgressions decreed upon one of them, or upon people in general — even disbelief itself — and they see that all of this unfolds by God's will, decree, and predestination, falling within the domain of His lordship and the dictates of His will. So they assume that surrendering to it, going along with it, and being content with it is itself religion, a spiritual path, and an act of worship. In this, they resemble the polytheists who said: "Had God willed, neither we nor our forefathers would have associated partners with Him, nor would we have forbidden anything." And they said: "Shall we feed those whom God could feed if He wished?" And they said: "Had the Most Merciful willed, we would never have worshipped them."

Had they been rightly guided, they would have known that we are commanded to accept divine decree and bear its consequences patiently only with regard to calamities that befall us — such as poverty, illness, and fear. God Almighty said: "No calamity befalls except by God's permission, and whoever believes in God, He will guide his heart." Some of the early scholars explained: "This refers to the man who is struck by a calamity and knows it is from God, so he accepts it and submits."

In the two authentic collections, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Adam and Moses debated. Moses said: 'You are Adam, whom God created with His own hand, breathed His spirit into, had His angels prostrate before, and taught the names of all things — so why did you cause us and yourself to be expelled from Paradise?' Adam replied: 'You are Moses, whom God chose for His messages and His speech — did you find that this was written against me before I was even created?' Moses said: 'Yes.' The Prophet said: 'So Adam prevailed over Moses in the argument.'"

Adam, peace be upon him, did not invoke divine decree against Moses thinking that a sinner can use predestination as an excuse — for no Muslim or rational person would say such a thing. Were that a valid excuse, it would equally excuse Iblis, the people of Noah, the people of Hud, and every disbeliever. Nor did Moses blame Adam for the sin itself, since Adam had already repented to his Lord, and God had chosen him and guided him. Rather, Moses blamed him for the calamity that befell them as a result of the transgression — which is why he said: "Why did you cause us and yourself to be expelled from Paradise?" Adam answered that this had been written against him before he was created. Thus both the deed and the resulting calamity were preordained, and what is preordained of calamities must be accepted with submission, for that is part of being fully content with God as one's Lord.

As for sins, the servant has no right to sin, and if he does sin, he must seek forgiveness and repent. So one repents from all manner of faults and bears calamities with patience. God Almighty said: "So be patient; indeed the promise of God is true, and seek forgiveness for your sins." He also said: "And if you are patient and mindful of God, their scheming will not harm you at all." And He said: "And if you are patient and mindful of God, that is indeed a matter of great resolve." And Joseph, peace be upon him, said: "Indeed, whoever fears God and is patient — then God does not allow the reward of the righteous to be lost."
