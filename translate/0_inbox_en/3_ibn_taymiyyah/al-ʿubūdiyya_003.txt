When a person recognizes that God is his Lord and Creator, and that he is entirely dependent upon Him and in need of Him, he comes to know the servitude that relates to God's lordship. Such a person asks his Lord, humbles himself before Him, and places his trust in Him. Yet he may at times obey God's commands and at other times disobey them; he may worship God, but he may also worship the devil and idols. This kind of servitude does not distinguish between the people of Paradise and the people of Hell, nor does it make a person a true believer — as God Almighty said: *"And most of them do not believe in God without associating others with Him."* The polytheists used to acknowledge that God was their Creator and Provider, yet they worshipped others besides Him.

Many of those who speak of spiritual reality and witness it perceive nothing beyond this reality — the cosmic reality that is shared in its witnessing and knowledge by believer and disbeliever, the righteous and the wicked alike. Even Iblis acknowledged this reality, as do the people of Hell. Iblis said: *"My Lord, grant me respite until the Day they are resurrected,"* and *"My Lord, because You have led me astray, I will surely make disobedience attractive to them on earth, and I will mislead them all,"* and *"By Your might, I will surely mislead them all."*

Whoever stops at this reality and its witnessing, without fulfilling what God has commanded of the religious reality — which is the worship connected to His divinity, and obedience to His command and the command of His Messenger — is of the same kind as Iblis and the people of Hell. If such a person then imagines himself to be among God's elite saints, the people of special knowledge and realization from whom the religious commandments and prohibitions have supposedly fallen away, he is among the worst of the disbelievers and heretics. And whoever believes that al-Khidr or anyone else was relieved of God's commands because of witnessing the divine will and the like — this claim is among the worst statements of those who disbelieve in God and His Messenger.

This remains so until a person enters into the second meaning of servitude — that of being a true worshipper — becoming one who worships God alone, worshipping none but Him, obeying His command and the command of His messengers, allying himself with His believing and God-fearing friends, and opposing His enemies. This worship is connected to the divinity of God Almighty, and this is why the declaration of monotheism is "There is no god but God" — in contrast to one who acknowledges God's lordship but does not worship Him, or worships another god alongside Him.

The true God is the one whom the heart devotes itself to with complete love, reverence, honor, respect, fear, hope, and the like. This is the worship that God loves and is pleased with, the worship with which He described His chosen servants, and for which He sent His messengers.
