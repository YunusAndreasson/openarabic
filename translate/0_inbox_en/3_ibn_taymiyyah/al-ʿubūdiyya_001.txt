The entire religion falls under the concept of worship. It is authentically established in the Sahih that when Gabriel came to the Prophet ﷺ in the form of a Bedouin and asked him about Islam, he said: "Islam is to testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, to establish prayer, to give alms, to fast during Ramadan, and to perform the pilgrimage to the House if you are able to find a way." He asked: "Then what is faith?" He replied: "To believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the resurrection after death, and to believe in divine decree — both its good and its evil." He asked: "Then what is excellence?" He replied: "To worship Allah as though you see Him, for even if you do not see Him, He surely sees you." Then, at the end of the hadith, he said: "This was Gabriel; he came to teach you your religion." So he made all of this part of the religion.

The word "religion" (dīn) carries the meaning of submission and humility. It is said: "I subjugated him, so he submitted" (dantuhū fa-dān). And it is said: "He devotes himself to Allah" and "he submits to Allah" — meaning he worships Allah, obeys Him, and humbles himself before Him. So the religion of Allah is His worship, obedience to Him, and submission to Him. The word "worship" (ʿibādah) likewise has humility at its root — a well-trodden path is called muʿabbad because feet have worn it down. However, the worship that is commanded encompasses both humility and love: it means the utmost humility before Allah combined with the utmost love for Him.

The highest degree of love is total devotion (tatīm), and its first stage is attachment (ʿalāqah), when the heart clings to the beloved; then comes yearning (ṣabābah), when the heart pours itself toward him; then infatuation (gharām), which is love that never leaves the heart; then passionate love (ʿishq); and the final stage is total devotion (tatīm). It is said: "taym Allāh" means "the slave of Allah," so the one utterly devoted (mutayyam) is enslaved to his beloved.

Whoever submits to a person while hating him is not truly worshipping him. And whoever loves something without submitting to it is not worshipping it either — just as a man may love his child or his friend. For this reason, neither one alone suffices for the worship of Allah, Exalted is He. Rather, Allah must be more beloved to the servant than anything else, and Allah must be greater in his sight than everything else. Indeed, none deserves complete love and submission except Allah. Whatever is loved for other than Allah — that love is corrupt. And whatever is revered without Allah's command — that reverence is false.

Allah, Exalted is He, says: *"Say: If your fathers, your sons, your brothers, your spouses, your clan, the wealth you have acquired, the commerce whose decline you fear, and the dwellings you find pleasing are dearer to you than Allah, His Messenger, and striving in His cause, then wait until Allah brings about His command."*

So the category of love belongs to Allah and His Messenger, just as obedience belongs to Allah and His Messenger, and seeking approval belongs to Allah and His Messenger. But worship — and what relates to it, such as reliance, fear, and the like — belongs to Allah alone.
