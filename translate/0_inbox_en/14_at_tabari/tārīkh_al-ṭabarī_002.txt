When God began creating Adam, the angels said among themselves, "God will never create anything more honored or more knowledgeable than us." And so they were tested through the creation of Adam — for every creation is tested.

God said to the angels, "I am placing a vicegerent on the earth." He told them what He intended to do, and they offered their opinion. He had taught them certain knowledge but withheld from them other knowledge that only He possessed. So based on what they did know, they said, "Will You place upon it one who will spread corruption and shed blood, while we glorify Your praises and sanctify You?" The angels had learned from God's knowledge that no sin was greater in His sight than the shedding of blood. God replied, "I know what you do not know" — for in God's knowledge, there would arise from that creation prophets, messengers, righteous people, and dwellers of Paradise.

When God began fashioning Adam, the angels whispered among themselves, "Let our Lord create whatever He wills — He will never create anything more knowledgeable than us or more honored in His sight." But when He created Adam and breathed into him of His spirit, He commanded them to prostrate before him, on account of what they had said, thereby elevating Adam above them. They then realized they were not superior to him, yet they said, "Even if we are not better than him, we are surely more knowledgeable, for we existed before him and nations were created before him."

When they grew proud of their knowledge, they were put to the test. God taught Adam the names of all things, then presented them before the angels and said, "Tell Me the names of these, if you are truthful — if indeed I never created anything without you being more knowledgeable than it, then tell Me their names, if you speak the truth."

The angels turned at once to repentance — as every believer does when confronted with the truth — and said, "Glory be to You! We have no knowledge except what You have taught us. You alone are the All-Knowing, the All-Wise."

God said, "Adam, tell them their names." And when Adam informed them of their names, God said, "Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and I know what you reveal and what you conceal?"

He had taught Adam the name of everything — the horses, the mules, the camels, the jinn, the wild beasts — and Adam named each thing by its name as every kind was presented before him.

God said, "Did I not tell you that I know the unseen of the heavens and the earth, and I know what you reveal and what you conceal?" As for what they revealed, it was their saying, "Will You place upon it one who will spread corruption and shed blood?" And as for what they concealed, it was what they said to one another: "We are better than him and more knowledgeable."

When Adam's superiority over them in knowledge became clear, they were filled with regret and turned to repentance and the acknowledgment of their own limitations. They understood then that God has a wisdom in His creation that none can fathom but He.
