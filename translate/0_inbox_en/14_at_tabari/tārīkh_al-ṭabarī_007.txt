**On Night and Day: Which Was Created First, and on the Beginning of the Creation of the Sun and Moon and Their Description, Since Time Is Known Through Them**

We have already discussed what God, mighty is His mention, created before He created times and seasons, and we have explained that times and seasons are merely the hours of night and day, and that these are simply the sun and moon traversing the degrees of the celestial sphere. So let us now address: which of the two came first — night or day? For there is disagreement on this matter among those who have examined it.

Some say: God created night before day. They argue for this by pointing out that when the sun sets and its light — which constitutes day — disappears, night immediately descends with its darkness. From this, it is clear that light is what encroaches upon night, and that night, unless dispelled by the day that encroaches upon it, is the constant, enduring state. This, they say, is evidence that night was the first to be created, and that the sun came after it. This view is attributed to Ibn Abbas.

Ibn Bashshar narrated to us, from Abd al-Rahman, from Sufyan, from his father, from Ikrimah, from Ibn Abbas, who said when asked whether night came before day: "Consider — when the heavens and the earth were a single joined mass, was there anything between them but darkness? From this you may know that night came before day."

Others said: Day came before night. They supported their view by arguing that God, mighty is His mention, existed when there was neither night nor day nor anything else besides Him, and that His light illuminated everything He created after its creation, until He created night.

Ali ibn Sahl narrated to me, from al-Hasan ibn Bilal, from Hammad ibn Salamah, from al-Zubayr Abu Abd al-Salam, from Ayyub ibn Abdullah al-Fihri, that Ibn Mas'ud said: "Your Lord has neither night nor day. The light of the heavens comes from the light of His face, and the measure of each of your days in His reckoning is twelve hours."

Abu Ja'far said: Of the two views, the one I consider more correct is that of those who said night came before day. This is because day, as I have mentioned, is the light of the sun, and God created the sun and set it in motion across the celestial sphere only after He had spread out the earth, as He said: *"Are you a more difficult creation or the sky? He built it, raised its vault and perfected it, darkened its night and brought forth its morning light."* If the sun was created only after the sky was raised and its night was darkened, then it is clear that before the sun was created and before God brought forth the sky's morning light, it was dark and not illuminated.

Furthermore, what we observe of night and day provides clear evidence that day is what encroaches upon night, for whenever the sun sets and its light vanishes — whether during the day or night — the sky darkens. This makes it clear that day is what encroaches upon night with its light and radiance. And God knows best.
