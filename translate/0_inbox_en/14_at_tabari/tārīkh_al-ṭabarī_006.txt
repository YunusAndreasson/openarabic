I was holding the hand of the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, as we walked together toward the west while the sun was sinking low. We kept watching it until it set. He said: I asked, "O Messenger of God, where does it set?" He replied: "It sets into the sky, then it is raised from one heaven to the next until it reaches the highest seventh heaven, where it comes to rest beneath the Throne. There it falls down in prostration, and the angels assigned to it prostrate alongside it. Then it says: 'My Lord, from where do You command me to rise — from where I set or from where I rise?' That is the meaning of His words, exalted be He: *'And the sun runs toward its resting place'* — where it is held beneath the Throne — *'That is the decree of the Almighty, the All-Knowing,'* meaning by that the handiwork of the Lord, Almighty in His dominion, All-Knowing of His creation." He continued: "Then Gabriel comes to it with a robe of light from the radiance of the Throne, measured to the hours of daylight — long in summer, short in winter, or somewhere in between during autumn and spring. It puts on that robe just as one of you puts on his clothes, then it travels through the expanse of the sky until it rises from its place of rising." The Prophet, peace be upon him, said: "It is as though it will be held back for the span of three nights, then it will not be clothed in light, and it will be commanded to rise from the west. That is the meaning of His words, exalted be He: *'When the sun is wrapped up.'*" He said: "And the moon is likewise — in its rising, its course across the horizon of the sky, its setting, its ascent to the highest seventh heaven, its confinement beneath the Throne, its prostration, and its seeking permission — except that Gabriel, peace be upon him, brings it a robe from the light of the Footstool." He said: "That is the meaning of His words, exalted be He: *'He made the sun a radiance and the moon a light.'*" Abu Dharr said: Then I turned back with the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, and we prayed the sunset prayer.

This report from the Messenger of God indicates that the reason for the difference between the sun and the moon is that the sun's light comes from a garment it is clothed in from the radiance of the Throne, while the moon's light comes from a garment it is clothed in from the light of the Footstool.

As for the other report that points to a different meaning: Muhammad ibn Abi Mansur narrated to me, saying: Khalaf ibn Wasil narrated to us, saying: Abu Nu'aym narrated to us, from Muqatil ibn Hayyan, from 'Ikrimah, who said: One day, while Ibn 'Abbas was sitting, a man came to him and said: "O Ibn 'Abbas, I heard something astonishing from Ka'b al-Ahbar about the sun and the moon." Ibn 'Abbas had been reclining, but he sat up straight and said: "What was it?" The man said: "He claimed that on the Day of Resurrection, the sun and the moon will be brought forth like two hamstrung bulls and cast into Hellfire."

'Ikrimah said: Ibn 'Abbas nearly flew apart with rage. Then he said: "Ka'b lied! Ka'b lied! Ka'b lied!" — three times. "This is nothing but a Jewish notion he wants to smuggle into Islam. God is far too majestic and noble to punish anyone for obeying Him. Have you not heard the word of God, blessed and exalted: *'And He subjected for you the sun and the moon, both constant'*? It means their constancy in obedience. How would He punish two servants He praises for being constant in His obedience? May God curse this rabbi and disgrace his rabbinical learning! How bold he is against God, and how monstrous his slander against these two obedient servants of God!"

He then repeated, "To God we belong and to Him we return," several times, and picked up a small twig from the ground and began poking it into the earth. He stayed like that for as long as God willed, then raised his head, tossed the twig aside, and said: "Shall I tell you what I heard the Messenger of God, peace be upon him, say about the sun and the moon — the beginning of their creation and their ultimate destiny?" We said: "Yes, may God have mercy on you!" He said: "The Messenger of God, peace be upon him, was asked about that, and he said: 'When God completed His creation to perfection and nothing remained to be created except Adam, He created two suns from the light of His Throne. As for the one He knew in His prior knowledge He would leave as a sun, He created it the size of the world, stretching from its easts to its wests.'"
