He (peace be upon him) was born on a Monday, on the second night of Rabīʿ al-Awwal — though some say the eighth, others the tenth, and others the twelfth. Al-Zubayr ibn Bakkār said he was born in Ramadan, but this is an anomalous view, as al-Suhaylī noted in his *al-Rawḍ*. His birth was in the Year of the Elephant, fifty days after it — though some say fifty-eight days. The sound position is that he was born in the Year of the Elephant, and Ibrāhīm ibn al-Mundhir al-Ḥizāmī, the teacher of al-Bukhārī, as well as Khalīfa ibn Khayyāṭ and others, reported consensus on this.

His father died while he was still in the womb — though some say a few months after his birth, others say a year, and others two years. The most well-known view is the first. A wet nurse was sought for him among the Banū Saʿd, and Ḥalīma al-Saʿdiyya nursed him, as we have related through a sound chain of narration. He stayed with her among the Banū Saʿd for about four years, and it was there that his chest was opened. She then returned him to his mother.

His mother set out with him to Medina to visit his maternal uncles there, but she passed away at al-Abwāʾ on the way back to Mecca. He was six years, three months, and ten days old at the time. Muslim reported in his *Ṣaḥīḥ* that when the Messenger of God (peace be upon him) passed by al-Abwāʾ on his way to Mecca in the Year of the Conquest, he asked his Lord's permission to visit his mother's grave. Permission was granted, and he wept, causing those around him to weep as well — and with him were a thousand armored men.

After his mother's death, Umm Ayman cared for him — she was his father's freed bondwoman whom he had inherited — and his grandfather ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib took charge of him. When the Messenger of God (peace be upon him) reached the age of eight, his grandfather died, having entrusted him to his uncle Abū Ṭālib, who was his father's full brother. Abū Ṭālib looked after him with the utmost care and defended him most staunchly when God sent him as a prophet — even though he himself remained upon polytheism until he died. God lightened his punishment on that account, as is confirmed in authentic tradition.

His uncle took him along on a trading journey to Syria when he was twelve years old. This was part of his uncle's complete devotion to him, since there was no one to look after the boy if he were left behind in Mecca. Along the way, his uncle and his companions witnessed remarkable signs concerning him (peace be upon him) that only increased his uncle's protectiveness. Al-Tirmidhī reported in his *Jāmiʿ*, with a chain of entirely trustworthy narrators, that a cloud shaded him, a tree leaned to cast its shadow over him, and the monk Baḥīrā foretold his future and urged his uncle to take him back, lest the Jews see him and do him harm. This account has a well-preserved basis.

He later traveled to Syria a second time, conducting trade on behalf of Khadīja bint Khuwaylid (may God be pleased with her), accompanied by her servant Maysara, under a profit-sharing arrangement. Maysara witnessed astonishing things about him and, upon returning, told his mistress what he had seen. She then expressed her desire to marry him, hoping for the good that God had gathered for her in this — and far beyond what any human mind could imagine. The Messenger of God (peace be upon him) married her when he was twenty-five years old.
